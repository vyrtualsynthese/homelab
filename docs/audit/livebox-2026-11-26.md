# The Livebox, measured — forwards, WAN and leases

> **In one line.** The house router's own port-forward table, WAN address and device list, read from
> the box on 2026-11-26 rather than remembered from a page.
>
> **Use it when** you need to know what the internet can reach without passing Traefik, which address
> the connection actually serves, or which machines share the `/24` · **Verified** 2026-11-26 ·
> **Kind:** record
>
> **Sources** the Livebox 6 at `192.168.1.1`, read over its SoftAtHome `sysbus` API with the admin
> account: `POST /ws` with `Content-Type: application/x-sah-ws-4-call+json`, one
> `sah.Device.Information.createContext`, then `DeviceInfo.get`, `NMC.get`,
> `Firewall.getPortForwarding` and `Devices.get`. Read-only; nothing was written to the box. The
> password is the operator's and is not stored in this repository.

## The forward table

Twelve rules, every one enabled, every one with hairpin NAT on ("hairpin": the public address also
works from inside the LAN). Protocol `6` is TCP, `17` is UDP; `6,17` means both. The names are the
box's own and two of them are wrong: it labels `5353` "FTP Server" and `32400` "FTP Data" from a
preset, and both are Plex-era artefacts.

Four of these are dead. `32400` and `32468` (TCP) are refused when dialled on `192.168.1.46`, and
`1900` and `5353` (UDP) answered neither a DLNA discovery search (SSDP) nor an mDNS query (the
discovery protocol printers and media servers speak). The cluster publishes none of the four: the
Traefik `Service` has exactly six ports — 80, 443, 25565, 22000 (TCP and UDP) and 21027 (UDP) — and
the Plex chart's `Service` has been `ClusterIP` since its old `NodePort` and DLNA ports were removed.
They are what remains of that Plex setup; the router's rules were not cleaned up with the chart.
`25565` is half-dead besides: the router forwards it on both protocols, but the Traefik `Service`
publishes it on TCP only, so the UDP half reaches nothing.

## The four dead rules, removed

The four were deleted the same day, over the same API: `Firewall.deletePortForwarding` with
`{"origin":"webui","id":…}` for `webui_FTP Data` (32400), `webui_PlexDlna` (32468),
`webui_PlexDlnaUDP` (1900) and `webui_FTP Server` (5353), each answering `{"status":true}`. A fresh
`getPortForwarding` then returned eight rules: 80 and 443 (TCP), 22000 (TCP and UDP), 21027 (UDP)
and 25565 (TCP and UDP) to `streaming`, and 51820, 51821 and 51822 to the two edge Pis. No other rule
was touched, and every surviving rule is still enabled.

## What the table changes

**1. The cluster's five forwarded ports are right, the page's wording was not.** The network page
listed `80, 443, 25565, 22000 (TCP and UDP) and 21027 (UDP)`. Measured, only 25565 and 22000 carry
both protocols: 80 and 443 are TCP only. The list is otherwise the set that reaches Traefik.

**2. "Traefik is the only door" is false as a statement about the router.** The box has seven rules
the network page did not mention. Four of them are dead (above). Three reach the two edge Pis and
never touch the cluster: the WireGuard family on 51820, 51821 and 51822, to `rbp1` and `rbp0`. In
practice Traefik is still the only door that answers HTTP(S), but the exposure model is the router's
twelve rules, not one cluster address.

**3. The `5000` question is answered: it is not forwarded.** The security baseline could not say
whether wg-easy's admin page, published on `5000/tcp` on both Pis, also answered the internet. It
does not — the table has no `5000` rule, and no `9902` (the smartctl exporter) either. The medium
finding in
[§2 of the baseline](../security/security-baseline-2026-11-24.md#2-the-vpn-and-the-screen-that-holds-it)
is unchanged — plain HTTP on `0.0.0.0` across the flat `/24` — but its exposure ends at the LAN.

**4. The LAPI's NodePort is safe, for a different reason than the plan gave.** The security-detection
plan graded `30500` as unreachable from the internet because "the router forwards five ports". It
forwards twelve, and `30500` is not one of them, so the grade holds and its stated reason does not.
That gap is exactly why this card existed.

**5. The `/24` is a household, not a rack.** The baseline's §3 finding assumes any device on the LAN
can reach the control plane. The lease list below confirms it: 30 addresses, 8 of them the homelab
itself, the rest televisions, phones, speakers, a printer and a set of Heatzy heaters. The box also
runs a separate guest network on `192.168.144.0/24` (`192.168.144.1` on the box), with no lease on it
at the read — the flat `/24` the baseline describes is the house's own, not a mixed one.

## What this record does not cover

- **Writing anything.** No rule was added, enabled, disabled or removed, and the box was not asked
  to export its backup file.
- **The firewall and the DMZ.** The rules above are the port-forward table only. The box's packet
  filter and any DMZ host are a separate read.
- **The guest network's content.** `192.168.144.0/24` exists and had no lease at the read; nothing on
  it was measured.
- **The WireGuard endpoints' liveness.** `51820`, `51821` and `51822` are UDP, which a TCP probe
  cannot confirm. The rules exist; whether the Pis' VPN answers is a different measurement.
- **Whether a host-level daemon on `streaming` answers `1900` or `5353`.** No probe answered, but
  silence on UDP is not proof: the cluster publishes neither port, which is what this record claims.
- **Anything after 2026-11-26.** The four dead rules were removed the same day, in the section above;
  the next change to the Plex chart, the router or the Pis moves the table again.
